Question :
there are 3 main tenses and sub tenses?

Yes! 👍 You're thinking about it correctly.

In the traditional English grammar system, there are 3 main time periods, and each has 4 common forms.

3 main tenses
Present
Past
Future
4 forms for each

| Tense | Present | Past | Future |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Simple** | I send | I sent | I will send |
| **Continuous** | I am sending | I was sending | I will be sending |
| **Perfect** | I have sent | I had sent | I will have sent |
| **Perfect Continuous** | I have been sending | I had been sending | I will have been sending |

That gives you the commonly taught 12 English tenses:

Present Simple
Present Continuous
Present Perfect
Present Perfect Continuous
Past Simple
Past Continuous
Past Perfect
Past Perfect Continuous
Future Simple
Future Continuous
Future Perfect
Future Perfect Continuous
A simple way to understand them

Think of it as:

3 times × 4 ways of describing an action = 12 tenses

For example, with send:

Present Simple: I send parcels.
Past Simple: I sent a parcel.
Future Simple: I will send a parcel.
Present Perfect: I have sent a parcel.

You don't need to memorize all 12 at once. Start with the simple tenses, then learn continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous one by one. That will make everything much easier. 😊
